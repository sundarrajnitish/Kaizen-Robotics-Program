Serial & Bluetooth Controlled Robots

Level 1 & 2 · UART

These robots are driven one character at a time over a serial link: a USB-TTL dongle from a PC terminal, or an HC-05 Bluetooth module from a phone app. The firmware waits for a byte, looks it up and sets the motors. The Bluetooth wheelchair also checks an ultrasonic sensor after every command and backs away from obstacles.

How it works

  1. The UART receives one byte at a time. In TTL.c, uart_receive() blocks until the RXC flag says a byte has arrived, then the switch sets PORTB.
  2. The motor pattern stays on until the next byte arrives, so a single key press keeps the robot moving.
  3. WC.c (wheelchair): after each command it fires a 10 µs trigger pulse at the HC-SR04 and times the echo with Timer1 input capture. Closer than 40 cm means stop for 1 s, then reverse for 1 s. The "Bluetooth RC Controller" phone app repeats the key while it is held, which keeps the distance checks going.
  4. Speed_Control_Assignment.ino: upper-case keys drive at analogWrite(255), lower-case at 100.
  5. Computer_Controlled.ino: when no byte is waiting it shows "Start" on the LCD and stops everything every 50 ms, so the robot only moves while the terminal keeps sending (hold the key down). Keys i/k and j/l move the arm.

Keyboard

Keys are sent as typed (Shift for upper case). Arrow keys send the forward/back/left/right character of the selected firmware.
